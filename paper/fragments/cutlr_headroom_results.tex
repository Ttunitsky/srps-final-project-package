Across all 54 instances the cutLR arm reduces mean certified gap by $0.105\pp$
and increases the refresh count from 32 to 35.  On the 25 confirmed-headroom
instances the reduction grows to $0.222\pp$ ($1.002\%\to 0.780\%$); these are
the cases where the standalone cutLR bound was already below the weakened
initial $ub$, so the in-loop refresh has room to fire even within its reduced
budget.  The 34 ties are instances the ceiling absorbs regardless: the weakened
initial $ub$ is already close to the ALNS incumbent, or the ALNS proves
optimality without a dual refresh.

\paragraph{Notable per-instance results.}
The largest cutLR win is \texttt{B\_n150\_030\_a25\_088}: LR records a
certified $ub=3561$ (gap $4.437\%$, zero refreshes) while cutLR-triple achieves
$ub=3551$ (gap $0.028\%$, one refresh), a difference of 10 profit units in the
certified bound.  The gap difference is amplified because LR's ALNS incumbent
regressed from a previous run (3550$\to$3403), a primal contamination artifact
of the sequential experiment design; the $ub$ difference of 10 is the clean
evidence.

The instances where plain LR wins (8 cases, all $\alpha=0.25$ hard families)
share a common pattern: accumulated warm-multiplier subgradient calls converge
to a tighter final Lagrangian value than cutLR's richer-but-slower steps reach
within the 30-second refresh cap.  This is the $\alpha=0.25$ tradeoff: sparse
job-processor overlap makes pair and triple incompatibilities rare, so the extra
penalty terms add overhead without meaningfully cutting the relaxation.
