The three cutLR configurations improve on every aggregate metric over
LR\_200\_cold; the triple configuration achieves the lowest mean gap
($0.361\%$, $-0.094\pp$ vs.\ baseline).

A decisive comparison is LR\_100$+$LR\_100 against cutLR-triple: both consume
the same total dual budget (100 cold then 100 warm iterations), but the warm
continuation without cuts achieves $0.467\%$ mean gap and wins only 19 of 54
instances versus the baseline, while cutLR-triple achieves $0.361\%$ and wins
30. The $+0.106\pp$ advantage of cutLR over the warm continuation cannot be
attributed to the warm start; it is the contribution of the cuts themselves.

The capacity-cut configuration (cutLR-cap) records the highest instance-win
count (35), reflecting that capacity tightness is widespread across the
benchmark. Adding pair incompatibilities (cutLR-pair) sharpens the per-instance
bound on medium-hard instances at the cost of a marginally slower convergence
per call, shifting some wins to losses on small-easy instances. The triple
configuration adds incompatible triples to the pair set, recovering the lowest
aggregate mean while the per-instance win count stays at 30.
